The WorldForge doctrine

Three laws. One foundation.

These laws turn a brand philosophy into system behaviors that can be reviewed, tested, and improved.

LAW I

Emergence is not canon.

A generated idea, discovered pattern, or compelling interpretation is a possibility—not an established truth. Candidates must pass the appropriate review and authorization process.

LAW II

Every claim answers to provenance.

Preserve where knowledge originated, what evidence supports it, who interpreted it, and what remains uncertain. Unsupported claims must not silently become facts.

LAW III

Evolution preserves continuity.

A world must change without erasing its history. Revisions, competing interpretations, rejected proposals, and authorized canon changes remain distinguishable.

Brand → system relationship

Different roles. One ecosystem.

The fictional universe inspires the system without becoming evidence for real-world claims. The system supports the universe without dictating every creative decision.

The WorldForge Constitution.

A foundational charter connecting epistemology, canon governance, system architecture, AI behavior, and creator rights.

01

Creator authority

The system may recommend; only properly authorized actors may approve governed changes.

02

Provenance

Significant claims should retain sources, evidence, interpretations, and attribution.

03

Uncertainty

Unknown, disputed, and unsupported states must remain visible rather than being disguised as certainty.

04

Reversibility & history

Preserve versions, rationales, and prior decisions so change does not erase the past.

05

Separation of roles

Observation, interpretation, proposal, decision, and canon are distinct operations.

06

Reviewable automation

AI assistance should expose its basis and limitations and must not silently exercise authority it has not been granted.

07

Continuity without stagnation

A world can evolve while retaining the traceable record of how it became what it is.

Central principle

The system may help a world discover what it could become.
It must not silently decide what that world is.
