WorldForge · Foundational doctrine

Three laws for a living world.

A world is not coherent because every detail stays frozen. It is coherent because change is traceable, claims are accountable, and authority is explicit.

I.
Emergence

Emergence is not canon.

A generated idea, discovered pattern, or compelling interpretation is a possibility—not an established truth. Candidate material must pass through the appropriate review and authorization process before becoming canon.

Operational implication: AI may suggest, compare, and prepare proposals. It may not silently promote its own output to authoritative truth.
II.
Provenance

Every claim must answer to its provenance.

Preserve where knowledge originated, what evidence supports it, who interpreted it, what conflicts with it, and what remains uncertain. The system should make unsupported or weakly supported claims visible rather than laundering them into facts.

Operational implication: every important claim can carry a source, evidence notes, confidence or uncertainty, and an interpretation history.
III.
Continuity

Evolution must preserve continuity.

A world must be able to change without erasing its history. Revisions, competing interpretations, rejected proposals, and authorized canon changes must remain distinguishable and reviewable.

Operational implication: version changes, decision records, and prior states remain accessible; no change is treated as if it had always been true.
