# Curriculum and Competency Framework

## Curriculum structure

The curriculum is organized around four connected dimensions:

| Dimension | Learner develops |
|---|---|
| Conceptual understanding | Vocabulary, architecture, principles, and system relationships |
| Operational capability | Ability to use workflows, tools, and documented procedures |
| Engineering capability | Ability to design, implement, test, and improve system components |
| Stewardship capability | Ability to preserve integrity, assess evidence, govern change, and act accountably |

## Competency levels

- **L0 — Orientation:** recognizes core terms, purpose, boundaries, and learning resources.
- **L1 — Foundation:** explains concepts and follows guided examples.
- **L2 — Applied:** performs bounded tasks and produces working artifacts with guidance.
- **L3 — Independent:** completes defined tasks, tests outcomes, and explains trade-offs.
- **L4 — Stewardship / Advanced:** designs or reviews systems, handles exceptions, and demonstrates accountable governance.

These are learning proficiency levels. They do not replace WorldForge's architecture maturity model or establish production assurance.

## Curriculum map

### AH-AIOS Core
System orientation; architecture and boundaries; identity and access; security and policy; audit and evidence; governance and controlled change.

### AI Harness
Model and agent concepts; tools and connectors; API integration; workflow orchestration; evaluation; reliability; safe operation and human oversight.

### WorldForge
Knowledge representation; entity and relationship modeling; sources and claims; evidence; provenance; canon lifecycle; version history; worldbuilding and continuity; trust and risk.

### Human Stewardship
Critical thinking; evidence assessment; uncertainty; responsible use; review practices; operational accountability; change discipline.

## Learning-objective format

Every module should state:
1. What the learner will be able to do.
2. Required prior knowledge.
3. Concepts and references.
4. Guided activity.
5. Independent exercise.
6. Evidence to submit.
7. Assessment criteria.
8. Known limitations and safety boundaries.

## Progression

Learners may enter at an appropriate level based on demonstrated prior knowledge. Completion of a lesson is not equivalent to demonstrated competency; competency requires assessed evidence.
