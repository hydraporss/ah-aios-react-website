## What to keep, what to reframe, what to drop

### Keep (grounded)
- The three-layer visual hierarchy (Universe / Hermes / Apps).
- The headline "Apps of a Universe."
- The conceptual alignment of the poster's lowest layer with WOF's world-model separation (fact/observation/inference/hypothesis/verified/canon).
- The presence of a governance/provenance layer in WOF (PROC-01, §2, §6, §7, §8, §9) — source traceability is genuinely the differentiator.
- The `CII_SOURCE_EVIDENCE_REGISTER.csv` and `CII_EXTERNAL_SOURCE_EVIDENCE_REGISTER.csv` as the authoritative "no CII source exists" records.

### Reframe
- "Simulation of a civilization OS" → "Aspirational position toward a civilization-scale knowledge system; WOF is the proposed architecture."
- "Apps vs. institutions" → "A widget inventory being reframed as a role-based system; the reframing is the WorldForge narrative, not the current surface."
- "Most worldbuilding software offers only notes/maps/charactersheets" → "Competitor landscape not audited; use only as unproven copy or remove."

### Drop / strongly caveat
- The 3-phase chronology as a fact — it is a forward narrative, not a recovery record.
- "The poster is the early blueprint of Chapter One" — an artifact is not a spec.
- Any claim that WorldForge is already implemented, tested, or canonized (it is not).
- Any conflation with CII (CII original NOT RECOVERED; VGCI-1.1 ≠ CII).

---

## One-line verdict

The pasted analysis is a **strong brand read** but its "WorldForge is the future" conclusion is branding, not evidence. The image genuinely anticipates the distinction between *worldbuilding tools* and *coherence-preserving systems*, and that signal maps cleanly onto WOF's proposed world model, knowledge categories, and governance boundary. What the analysis cannot support — and what must be labeled as such — is any claim that WorldForge is implemented, tested, canonized, or CII-equivalent at this moment.
