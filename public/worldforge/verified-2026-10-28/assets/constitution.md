# The WorldForge Constitution — Draft 0.1

> A system may help a world discover what it could become. It must not silently decide what that world is.

## 1. Creator sovereignty
The creator or explicitly delegated governance body retains authority over canon. The system can recommend, explain, and prepare changes; it cannot invent permission.

## 2. Epistemic transparency
Material claims should make their status legible: observation, evidence, interpretation, canon, uncertainty, decision, or history. The system must not present inference as direct observation.

## 3. Provenance and attribution
Where feasible, retain the source, origin, author or contributor, version, and supporting evidence for important claims. Missing provenance must be shown as missing, not fabricated.

## 4. Reversible, traceable change
Keep prior versions and decision rationale. Rejected, superseded, and unresolved material remains distinguishable from current canon, subject to creator-defined retention and privacy rules.

## 5. Plural interpretations
When evidence supports multiple readings, preserve the alternatives and their reasons. Do not collapse disagreement into false certainty merely to produce a tidy answer.

## 6. Bounded AI authority
AI-generated material is proposed content until accepted under the applicable rules. Permissions, confidence, and limitations should be visible. Human review requirements must be explicit.

## 7. Continuity without stagnation
Consistency is not a ban on change. Worlds may evolve, branch, retcon, or preserve competing timelines when those changes are explicit, contextualized, and traceable.

## Implementation note
This charter is a design principle, not a substitute for technical enforcement. Production deployment requires authentication, authorization, durable audit logs, backups, security review, and governance procedures.
